HDDSynthPro emulates an IDE hard disk (and a CD-ROM drive) from a microSD card. This release adds firmware for the **HDDSynthPro PCB** (rev 0.3), which reads and writes the SD card about four times faster than before. Nothing changes on the Olimex RP2350-PICO2-BB48: its firmware is the same as v0.30.0's.

## Which file to flash

Put the board in its bootloader (hold BOOTSEL while plugging it in, or type `b` on the serial console), then copy one UF2 onto the drive that appears.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `hddsynthpro-pcb-drive-v0.31.0.uf2` | **New: the hard disk drive for the HDDSynthPro PCB**, with the SD card on 4-bit SDIO. Start here if you have the PCB. |
| `hddsynthpro-bb48-drive-v0.31.0.uf2` | The hard disk drive for the BB48 (the card on SPI). It also runs on the PCB, more slowly. |
| `hddsynthpro-bb48-hdcd-v0.31.0.uf2` | *Exploratory.* A hard disk and a CD-ROM drive as device 1 (BB48 or PCB; the card on SPI). |
| `hddsynthpro-bb48-cdrom-v0.31.0.uf2` | *Exploratory.* A CD-ROM drive on its own (BB48 or PCB; the card on SPI). |
| `hddsynthpro-bb48-listen-v0.31.0.uf2` | Listen only, for recording a real drive's bus; never drives a pin. |

## New

- **The SD card at about four times the speed, on the PCB.** The PCB's firmware talks to the card over its 4-bit bus (SDIO) instead of SPI. Through a USB IDE adapter in multiword DMA, from an image on the card: reads 6.2-6.6 MB/s (1.4 over SPI), random 4 KB reads about 680 a second (285), writes 5.8-7.2 MB/s (2.0). It ran 10 hours writing and reading an image with every sector checked: 54.7 GB written, 83.3 GB read, no errors. A retro PC's own bus is slower than either, so there the difference shows mostly in writes and while the card is busy.
- **The board's checks make room.** The card's 4-bit bus uses one of the chip's three PIO blocks, so the drive decides at start-up what else runs. Serving an image with DMA, the on-cable data check and the IORDY timing are off (the fastest set-up); without DMA they run; serving the built-in test disk, the card waits and everything runs. `S` says what runs and why.
- **A card left in SPI mode is noticed.** A card used by other firmware (the BB48's, or `bb48_drive`) stays in SPI mode until its power is cut. The PCB's firmware then uses it over SPI and `S` says so; take the card out and put it back, and the drive restarts onto the faster bus by itself.

## Fixed

- The SDIO driver (a copy of SdFat's, with fixes): after a status check in the middle of a read, SdFat's returned other sectors' data with no error; it freed state machines it didn't own and left two running when it stopped. None of this affects the BB48 firmware, which doesn't use it.

## Known limits

- Everything in v0.30.0's notes still applies.
- On the PCB, don't take the card out while the drive is running unless you mean to: it recovers when it can, but the computer may have lost what it was writing.
- The card runs at 25 MHz; 50 MHz would need the card switched to high-speed mode first, and gains little through today's hosts.
- The CD-ROM builds keep the card on SPI, on the PCB too.
