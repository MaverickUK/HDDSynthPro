IDE Synth Bay (formerly HDD Synth Pro) emulates an IDE hard disk and a CD-ROM drive from a microSD card. In this release the drive sets itself up: it learns the PC it's plugged into and picks the fastest way to work with it, including DMA, with no settings. It also works with the HDD Synth: the synth plays what the drive is doing, and the drive can run at the pace of the drive the synth is playing.

## Which file to flash

Put the board in its bootloader (hold BOOTSEL while plugging it in, or type `b` on the serial console), then copy one UF2 onto the drive that appears.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `hddsynthpro-pcb-drive-v0.35.0.uf2` | The drive for the **IDE Synth Bay PCB**: hard disk, CD-ROM drive or both, the SD card on 4-bit SDIO. |
| `hddsynthpro-bb48-drive-v0.35.0.uf2` | The same for the **Olimex RP2350-PICO2-BB48** (the card on SPI). It also runs on the PCB, more slowly. |
| `hddsynthpro-bb48-listen-v0.35.0.uf2` | Listen only, for recording a real drive's bus; never drives a pin. |

## Added

- **The drive sets itself up** (`bus = auto` and `dma = auto`, now the defaults). Every time the PC starts or resets, the drive begins in its safest way of answering the IDE bus. As the PC boots it learns what it's dealing with: an old ISA card, or a later controller, and whether the controller waits for the drive when asked to. Once it knows the PC can take it, the drive switches to its fastest way of answering, while running, and offers DMA. On an Athlon XP PC, Windows 98 found DMA on its very first boot, with no settings at all. A settings file that already chooses a way of answering (`early_answers`, `register_table`) or sets `dma = on` or `off` keeps doing what it says. A CD-ROM drive still needs `dma = on` for DMA, as before.
- **The HDD Synth link, both ways.** With an HDD Synth connected (three wires; see [docs/activity-link.md](https://github.com/MaverickUK/HDDSynthPro/blob/main/docs/activity-link.md)), the synth plays the drive's sequential and random activity, and seeks that follow where the head goes. With `seek_timing = synth` the drive also runs at the pace of whichever drive the synth is playing, from a 1985 stepper drive to a 2005 7,200 rpm one, and changes when you change the synth's pack. The synth needs its firmware v0.24.1 or later.
- **Seek timing models the drive's cache:** reading ahead, and writes kept in the cache and written out later, as real drives did. Copying a large folder under Windows 98 with the 2005 drive's timing took 55 s, against 1 min 54 s without the cache (and 51 s with no timing at all).

## Changed

- **The SD card's high-speed mode is on by default** (the PCB). It checks itself at every start and goes back to the normal speed by itself if the card can't keep up or gives a bad read. `sd_high_speed = off` turns it off.

## Fixed

- The drive's own check of the data on the cable reported false errors on disk images read with block mode (it lost its place; the data was right).

## Tested

- **The Athlon XP PC (Windows 98 SE):** the default settings learned the PC in about 10 s and Windows used DMA from its first boot; copying `C:\WINDOWS` (184 MB) within the disk took 51 s. The benchmark disk passed, every sector checked. The CD-ROM with `dma = on`.
- **The HDD Synth Mk5 (v0.24.1)** on the same PC: activity and head positions followed, and the drive's timing following the synth's pack.
- **Through a USB IDE adapter:** the drive switching its way of answering a thousand times under the read and write tests with no command lost, and DMA after learning (6.8 MB/s reads, 13.2 MB/s writes); not the full set of tests on this exact build.

## Known limits

- **New defaults, few PCs so far.** The automatic set-up has run on one retro PC (the Athlon XP) and one USB adapter. Not yet on a Pentium with an Intel PIIX controller or an ISA card. If a PC misbehaves, put `bus = early` and `dma = off` in `HDDSYNTH.INI` for the previous behaviour, and please report it.
- **High-speed mode is on by default before its long card soak.** If your card gives trouble, `sd_high_speed = off`.
- The first boot of an older Windows that reads the disk's details very early could still find DMA only after a restart.
- Everything in v0.34.0's notes still applies.
