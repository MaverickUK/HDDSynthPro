Olimex BB48 to a 40-pin IDE header

26 signal wires and the grounds. No power crosses between the boards: they share ground only. Pick any pin or row to see its wire.

Use a plain BB48, not the BB48R

On the R, GPIO 8 is the PSRAM chip-select and GPIO 9–11 and 24 go to its SD slot. Here those pins carry DD4–DD7 and CS1#.

Power order

BB48 on (USB-C) before the adapter's USB is plugged in; adapter unplugged before the BB48. Its pins are 5 V tolerant only while powered. The adapter drives the IDE lines whenever it is powered, even with nothing powered at the drive end, so an unpowered BB48 would be fed through its pins.

One strip per header pin

Before soldering the IDC helper, check with a meter that pins 1 and 2 (RESET# and GND) are on different Perma-Proto strips.

Leave these unconnected

On the Perma-Proto

  • Solder female headers for the BB48 rather than the board itself, so it can be swapped for your spare.
  • Seat it across the centre channel at one end, with the EXT1 row (GPIO 0–23) facing the IDC helper at the other end. 21 of the 27 signals are on EXT1.
  • Put the two 33 Ω resistors in DIOR# and DIOW#, close to the BB48.
  • Run the IDE grounds and the BB48's GND to the same ground rail. Wire all seven IDE grounds if you can, and at least three spread along the header.
  • Keep signal wires short, under 15 cm.
  • Leave GPIO 32–37 free for the SD breakout in slice 5.

First power-up check

With the adapter powered and idle, type p in the serial monitor. You should see something close to this:

PINS DD=.... DA=. CS0#=1 CS1#=1 DIOR#=1 DIOW#=1 IORDY=1 INTRQ=0 RESET#=1
  • Strobes, chip selects and RESET# are high (idle).
  • IORDY is high: the host pulls it up to 5 V.
  • INTRQ and DD7 are low: the host pulls them down.
  • The other DD and DA lines may float.

A strobe or chip select reading 0 at idle usually means a wire is on the wrong pin.
